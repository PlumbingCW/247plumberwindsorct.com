Emergency Water Line Repair in Windsor, CT

Water line repair in Windsor, CT covers the buried pipe between the street and your home. When it fails you lose pressure, or lose water, and often find a wet patch in the yard.

Call (860) 397-8103

Your water service line is the pipe that runs underground from the utility connection to the house. When it breaks, the first sign is often a drop in pressure, brown water, a hissing sound near the meter, or a patch of lawn that stays soggy in dry weather. If the water has stopped entirely or the yard is flooding, call (860) 397-8103. Emergency dispatch is 24/7.

While you wait, close the main shutoff inside the house to stop the flow into the building. If water is bubbling up in the yard, keep children and pets away and avoid driving over the wet area.

What a failing service line looks like

Signs the line outside is failing

  • ✓
    Pressure that drops for no reason Fixtures run weaker, but your inside plumbing is fine.
  • ✓
    A wet or sunken patch in the yard Water follows the pipe trench and surfaces where the ground is lowest.
  • ✓
    A meter that spins when everything is off Water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and the house.
  • ✓
    Discolored or gritty water Corroded pipe or a damaged connection lets sediment in.

Any one of these is worth a call. Two together usually means the line needs attention.

Repair or replace

A single failure on a sound line is a repair. We locate the break, excavate that section and make a clean connection. When the pipe is old, pitted or has failed more than once, replacement costs less over time than repeated digging. Common replacement materials are copper and polyethylene service tubing, and the right one depends on soil, depth and the existing connections.

The tradeoff is disruption. Open trenching is direct and reliable, but it cuts lawns, walks and sometimes driveways. We explain where the line runs, what would be disturbed and what restoration is your responsibility before work begins.

Windsor specifics: who owns what

Windsor homes get water from the MDC. Responsibility for the line is usually split, with the utility maintaining its main and connection and the homeowner responsible for the pipe on their side. Where one ends and the other begins varies, so call the MDC and ask where its responsibility stops before you hire a contractor to dig. We can help you work out which side the failure is on.

Know what is underground before anyone digs. Call 811 first so utilities mark their lines, and keep the markings visible until the work is done. In older parts of town, around Windsor Center and along Palisado Avenue, lines can run in unexpected places, and records are not always complete.

Low water pressure

Weak pressure at every fixture points to the line, the meter, the main shutoff or a pressure regulator. If pressure is low only at hot taps, look at the water heater. If it dropped suddenly, a partly closed valve, a failing regulator or a leak in the service line is likely. We check pressure with a gauge at an outdoor spigot, trace it back and tell you what we find.

Ground conditions and the river towns

Soil near the Farmington and Connecticut Rivers can stay wet, which speeds corrosion on older metal lines. Town planning documents also flag erodible soils on the Farmington River banks, and we watch for undermined trenches in those areas. If you live near a brook such as Mill, Meadow or Deckers, tell us. Wet ground changes how we dig and how we support the new line.

If a leak is suspected but unconfirmed, we can start with leak detection to confirm the line before opening the ground.

After the work

We pressure test the new line, check pressure at the fixtures and confirm the meter stops with everything off. You get a walk-through of where the line runs and where the shutoff sits. Permit rules for line replacement vary, so ask the Windsor building department what applies to your property.
