Emergency Water Heater Repair in Windsor, CT

Water heater repair in Windsor, CT starts with figuring out which part failed. Many no-hot-water problems are a small fix, and we handle them 24/7.

When the hot water stops, check the easy things first. For an electric heater, look at the breaker and the reset button on the upper thermostat. For gas, look at the pilot light and make sure the gas valve is on. If those do not help, call (860) 397-8103. When a water heater problem is an emergency

Water pooling around the base, scalding temperatures at the tap, a rotten-egg or gas smell or a relief valve that keeps discharging all deserve prompt attention. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first. For leaks, shut off the cold water to the unit and call us.

Do not ignore a relief valve that drips. It is a safety device, and a constant drip can mean excessive pressure or temperature, which the unit should not reach.

Gas, electric and hybrid units

Gas heaters rely on a pilot or igniter, a thermocouple or flame sensor, a gas control valve and a draft. Electric units use two heating elements and thermostats. Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor, fan, condensate drain and electronics, so they need a technician comfortable with refrigerant systems.

We repair all three types. We tell you plainly when a repair is not worth the cost, which is usually when the tank is leaking, or when the unit is old and parts are failing one after another.

Why Windsor water heaters get sediment and corrosion

Water chemistry affects tanks. Windsor is served by the MDC, which publishes an annual water quality report you can request or read online. Water that carries minerals leaves sediment at the bottom of the tank, and the anode rod inside sacrifices itself to protect the steel. Once the rod is consumed, the tank starts to corrode.

We do not guess your water's hardness. If you want a number, check the MDC's water quality report or test your tap. Either way, an annual flush and an anode rod check extend a tank's life, and rust-colored hot water is a prompt to inspect it.

Preventive checks you can do

Once a year, drain a few gallons from the drain valve into a bucket to clear loose sediment. Test the relief valve by lifting the lever briefly, and make sure it reseats. Look for rust on the tank base and corrosion at fittings. If you are not comfortable with any step, we will do it for you.

If your unit is old, or if repair costs are piling up, see water heater replacement for options. For planned upgrades, water heater installation covers a clean new setup.
