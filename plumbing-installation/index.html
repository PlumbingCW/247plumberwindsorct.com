Rough-in, then finish

Plumbing work comes in two stages. The rough-in is everything inside the walls and under the floor, including supply lines, drains and vents. After inspection, the walls close, and the finish stage sets fixtures and connects trim. If rough-in is wrong, everything built on top of it has to be reopened.

We measure fixture locations, check framing for notches and plan for access. Tubs and showers need a clear path for the drain, and kitchen islands need vent strategies since a standard wall vent is not available.

Venting and drainage

Every fixture drain needs air to flow. A vent line lets air into the system and carries sewer gases out through the roof. Poor venting causes gurgling, slow drains and trap siphoning. We design the venting to code and explain it in plain terms.

Basement bathrooms often sit below the sewer line, so they need an ejector pump or an up-flush system. Those have their own maintenance needs, and we tell you what they involve before you commit.

Permits, inspections and Windsor rules

Plumbing work usually requires a permit and an inspection. Rules vary by job and by town, so ask the Windsor building department what applies. Gas piping and some other work can require a licensed professional. Whoever you hire, you can verify a Connecticut plumbing license on the state's eLicense lookup, run by the Department of Consumer Protection at elicense.ct.gov.

Windsor limits new structures in 100-year floodplains, so additions near the Farmington River or a brook may face extra rules. Plan early if your lot sits near water.

Water softeners, filters and gas piping

Water treatment equipment connects to the same supply lines we install. Softeners, filters and conditioners need a drain, a bypass and an electrical outlet. We do not assume what your water needs. Test your tap, ask your water utility for its annual water quality report and choose equipment from the results.

Gas piping is safety work. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anyone else. A licensed professional should install or alter gas lines, and permits are typically required, so ask the Windsor building department what applies.

Related work

Water heater swaps are covered in water heater installation. If an install uncovers corroded supply lines, repiping can be added while the walls are open. Disposal and sink upgrades are on our garbage disposal and faucet pages.

Code basics for new plumbing work

New fixtures, lines and drains follow a published standard. Connecticut's State Building Code uses the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as its plumbing standard. The 2022 state code adopts the 2021 IPC, and the state is working on a newer edition. Ask the Windsor building department which permit your project needs. Source: Connecticut Office of the State Building Inspector.
