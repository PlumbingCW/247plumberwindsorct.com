Whole-House Repiping in Windsor, CT

Whole-house repiping in Windsor, CT replaces worn supply lines throughout your home. It is the long-term fix for rusty water, weak pressure and leaks that keep coming back.

Call (860) 397-8103

Repiping replaces the water supply pipes inside a house, from the main entry to each fixture. It is a major job, so it makes sense only when repair has stopped being enough. If you have a burst pipe now, see burst pipe repair. If you are weighing a bigger fix, call (860) 397-8103 and a plumber will talk through your options.

Signs a house needs repiping are specific. Rust-colored or metallic-tasting water, pressure that drops across the house, multiple leaks in a few years, or pipes that show green crust or white scale all point that way.

How a repipe is done

We map the layout, plan access points and protect floors and furniture. Most repipes use small openings in walls and ceilings, since PEX can snake through framing. Existing pipes are cut off, new lines are run to each fixture, and connections are made at valves and appliances. We test the new system before closing access holes.

Water is off for part of the day, so plan accordingly. We stage the work so you keep as much water as possible, and we explain how long the job should take for your layout.

Drains, cast iron and other pipes

Repiping usually refers to supply pipes. Drain, waste and vent lines are a separate system. Cast iron drains in older homes can crack, rust through or clog with scale, and a camera shows their condition. If a drain stack or a buried line is the problem, ask about sewer line repair or plumbing installation, which covers drain rerouting during a remodel.

A repipe is a good time to deal with other issues. We can add shutoff valves at fixtures, replace the main shutoff and install a pressure regulator if pressure is high.

Windsor houses and what we find

Windsor has houses from many eras, and the plumbing inside them reflects each decade's habits. Older homes near Windsor Center and along Palisado Avenue may carry galvanized or early copper, and later additions may mix materials. Mixed metals can corrode where they meet if the right dielectric fittings are not used.

We do not guess hardness or water chemistry for your tap. The MDC publishes its water quality report, and a test gives you a clearer picture. Water quality and pipe material together decide what lasts longest in your house.

Planning for the days of the job

Clear access to the basement, utility room and any walls where we will work. Move furniture away from wall access points and cover items that are sensitive to dust. Plan for the water to be off for part of each working day, and keep a supply of drinking water on hand.

Tell us about any past leaks, patched pipes or remodels. That history helps us find hidden runs and avoid surprises. After the repipe, we test pressure, check each fixture and walk you through the main shutoff and any new valves.

The code behind a repipe

Whole-house repiping replaces supply lines that the plumbing code governs. Connecticut's State Building Code uses the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as its plumbing standard. The 2022 state code adopts the 2021 IPC, and the state is working on a newer edition. Ask the Windsor building department about permits and inspections for your scope.
