When a boiler failure is an emergency

No heat in freezing weather, water leaking from the boiler or piping, a smell of fuel or a boiler that shuts down repeatedly all deserve a prompt call. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911. If you suspect carbon monoxide, get fresh air first.

Heating oil leaks are also serious. Do not try to clean them up yourself. Call the right authorities and your fuel supplier.

Hydronic systems in older Windsor homes

Hot-water baseboard and radiator systems are common in older New England housing, including homes near Windsor Center, Poquonock and Wilson. These systems use zone valves or circulators to move heated water through the house. The most frequent failures are circulators, zone valves, expansion tanks and air in the lines.

Boilers last a long time when maintained. An annual service helps catch small problems before the first cold snap. If you hear gurgling, see rust at fittings or notice uneven heat, schedule a visit before winter, not during it.

Pressure, expansion tanks and relief valves

A hot-water boiler runs under pressure, and the expansion tank gives heated water room to expand. When the tank loses its air charge, pressure spikes and the relief valve may discharge. We check the tank and the fill valve, set the right pressure and test the relief valve.

A dripping relief valve is a symptom, not a nuisance. Never cap it. The valve is protecting the system from overpressure.

Boiler or water heater problem?

Some homes use the boiler to heat domestic hot water as well. If your showers go cold at the same time as the radiators, the boiler is the likely source. If only the taps are cold, the problem may be an indirect tank or a separate unit, and water heater repair applies.

When a boiler is at the end of its life, we explain replacement choices and the plumbing changes that come with them, which we can handle alongside the heating contractor.

Getting ready for the heating season

Before the first cold week, check the boiler for leaks and rust, confirm the pressure on the gauge sits in the range marked for your system and bleed any radiators that stay cold at the top. Make sure the expansion tank has air and that the zone valves open and close.

Keep the area around the boiler clear. Know where the emergency switch and the shutoff valves are. Having a plumber check the system on a mild day costs less in stress than a call on the coldest night of the year.

What to do when a boiler problem is a safety problem

If you smell gas, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the building at once and call 911 from outside. Do not turn lights on or off, and do not try to relight a pilot. Call the gas utility from a safe place. Boiler trouble with no gas smell and no alarm can wait for a plumber, but a hissing sound, a water leak near the burner or a tripped breaker should be treated carefully.

Do not open a boiler's pressure relief valve to test it unless you know how, and do not run a boiler that is leaking or making unusual noise. Shut the unit off, close its water feed if you can reach it safely and call. Heat can be restored. A cracked heat exchanger or a flooded burner room cannot be rushed.

Code reference for boiler work

Boiler work involves gas piping, venting and water connections. Connecticut's State Building Code uses the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as its plumbing standard. The 2022 state code adopts the 2021 IPC, and the state is working on a newer edition. Ask the Windsor building department which permits apply.
