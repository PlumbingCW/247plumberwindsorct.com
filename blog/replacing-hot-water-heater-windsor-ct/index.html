Replacing a Hot Water Heater in Windsor, CT

Replacing a hot water heater is easier when you plan it. This guide covers signs it is time, the choices to make and what happens on the day.

Call (860) 397-8103

By the Windsor 24/7 Plumbing team · Published September 22, 2026 · Updated September 22, 2026

Most water heaters give some warning before they fail. Replacing one on your schedule is cheaper and calmer than replacing it after the tank floods the basement. If you are wondering whether it is time, this guide walks through the signs, the choices you will face and what happens when the plumber arrives.

Signs it is time to replace

Warning signs

  • ✓
    Age over ten years Many tanks last about ten to fifteen years. A unit in that range deserves attention.
  • ✓
    Rust-colored hot water It can mean the tank is corroding, though the anode rod may also be the cause.
  • ✓
    Rumbling or popping Sediment has hardened on the bottom.
  • ✓
    Moisture or rust at the base A tank that weeps is near failure.
  • ✓
    Not enough hot water The unit may no longer keep up.

Any of these is a reason to have the unit looked at.

Decisions to make before the plumber comes

Fuel comes first. If your home has gas, a gas heater is usually the simplest swap. If it is all electric, a standard electric tank or a hybrid heat pump unit are the choices. Size comes next. Choose a capacity that matches your household, and remember that a bigger tank costs more to keep hot.

Type is the third decision. A standard tank is the lowest-cost, quickest swap. A tankless unit saves space and gives continuous hot water. A hybrid reduces electric use but needs room and airflow.

What replacement day looks like

The plumber shuts off the water and fuel, drains the old tank and disconnects the unit. The old heater leaves the house. The new unit is set in place on a pan or stand, connected to the water lines with new fittings and to the gas or electric supply. For gas models, venting is checked or replaced.

A relief valve with a discharge pipe is installed. If the system needs an expansion tank, it goes in. The tank is filled, air is bled from the lines, the burner or element is started, and everything is checked for leaks. The plumber walks you through the controls and the shutoff valves.

Replacing a heater in a Windsor basement

Many water heaters sit in basements, and some Windsor basements are in areas the town flags for flooding. The town's hazard planning documents list Batchelder Road, Pleasant Street and River Street among areas at risk of flooding, and the town notes it improved Meadow Brook with a new road culvert in 2007 and bank stabilization in 2011. If your home is near one of these areas, raise the new unit on a stand and keep controls away from the floor.

Gas and electrical work needs licensed professionals, and permit rules vary. Ask the Windsor building department what applies to a replacement. You can also verify any Connecticut plumber's license on the Department of Consumer Protection's eLicense lookup.

Source type: Capitol Region Council of Governments hazard mitigation plan annex for Windsor and the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection.

Repair first, or just replace?

A new thermostat or element on a young tank is worth doing. A leaking tank is not worth repairing, because the steel liner cannot be patched. If your unit is old and has already needed a repair, replacement usually makes more sense. Our water heater repair page explains what we can fix, and water heater replacement covers the swap.

If you want to compare options and plan a layout change, see water heater installation.

Plan for the disruption

Expect the water to be off for part of the day, and plan for no hot water until the tank heats. A standard tank takes a while to recover after filling. Clear a path to the unit, move stored items and secure pets. If you are in a neighboring town, Bloomfield and South Windsor homes get the same service.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not reuse a corroded vent or old supply connectors on a new heater. They are inexpensive to replace and expensive to ignore. Do not skip the relief valve discharge pipe. It should run to a safe spot and never be capped. Do not buy a larger tank than you need, since you pay to keep that water hot every day.

Register the warranty as soon as the unit is installed and keep the paperwork where you can find it. Note the install date on the tank. A year from now you will be glad you did, and so will whoever owns the house next.

After the new heater is in

Run hot water at several taps for a few minutes to clear air and sediment from the lines. Check the area around the heater after an hour and again the next day for drips. Set the thermostat at a safe temperature and keep records of the settings.

Test the relief valve once a year by lifting the lever briefly, and flush a few gallons from the tank drain every year or so. A little attention keeps a new heater running efficiently for years.
