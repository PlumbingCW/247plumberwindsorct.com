Cured-in-Place Pipe Lining in Windsor, CT

Cured-in-place pipe lining, often shortened to CIPP, repairs a damaged sewer line from the inside. Here is how it works and when it fits a Windsor home.

Call (860) 397-8103

By the Windsor 24/7 Plumbing team · Published September 8, 2026 · Updated September 8, 2026

Cured-in-place pipe lining is a way to repair a cracked or root-infested sewer line without digging up the whole yard. A felt tube soaked in resin is pulled or inflated into the old pipe, then hardened with steam, hot water or light. The result is a new pipe inside the old one. It is a good fit for some Windsor homes and a poor fit for others, and the difference comes down to the condition of the existing pipe.

How the lining process works

Lining starts with cleaning. The pipe has to be clear of roots, scale and debris so the liner can bond smoothly. We clean with a cable or with hydro jetting, then send a camera through to confirm the pipe is ready.

Next, a flexible tube saturated with epoxy resin is pushed through an access point, usually a cleanout or a small excavation at one end. It is inflated against the pipe wall and cured. After it hardens, we reopen any side connections with a cutter, then run a final camera pass to check the result.

What lining can repair

Lining works well on cracks, joint offsets that are minor, root intrusion at joints and general deterioration along a straight run of pipe. It seals the leak points that let roots in, which solves the cause of many repeat clogs.

It does not reduce the pipe's flow much, since the liner is thin. The inside diameter shrinks slightly, but the smooth surface often moves water as well as the old pipe did.

When lining is not the answer

A pipe that has collapsed, that has a severe belly holding water or that has major offsets cannot be lined. The liner needs a clear path and a sound enough host pipe to carry it. In those cases, open-trench replacement or pipe bursting is the better option, and our sewer line repair page compares them.

Connections matter too. A line with several sharp bends, or with fittings that cannot be cleared, can block the process. A camera visit before any decision keeps you from paying for a method that will not fit.

What Windsor property owners should know first

Windsor is an MDC member town, which means the MDC supplies water and handles wastewater services. Your lateral from the house to the street connection is usually your responsibility, and the main in the street is the utility's. Before you commit to any sewer repair, ask the MDC where its responsibility starts for your address.

Digging also has local limits. Windsor's town ordinance restricts new structures in 100-year floodplains, and the town's hazard documents note erodible soils on the Farmington River banks. If your lateral runs through wet or unstable ground, a lining job can avoid disturbing it. Call 811 before any excavation, and ask the Windsor building department what permits apply to your work.

Questions to ask any contractor about lining

Ask for the camera footage that shows why lining is appropriate. Ask what the liner is made of, how long the warranty runs and who stands behind it. Ask how they plan to reopen side connections and what happens at the ends of the liner.

Ask also what the alternative would be. A contractor who offers only one method is not comparing your options. You can check any Connecticut plumber's license through the Department of Consumer Protection's eLicense lookup before you hire.

What to do next

If your sewer line backs up repeatedly, start with a camera inspection. If the camera shows a crack or root intrusion on a straight run, lining may be worth a quote. If it shows a collapse, plan for digging. For neighboring towns, see our pages for South Windsor and Bloomfield, where we also inspect and repair sewer lines.

Questions to settle before you sign

Ask who will pull the permit, if one is needed, and who schedules the inspection. Ask how long the sewer will be out of service, since you cannot use water while the liner cures. Ask how the crew will keep sewage out of the house during the work, and where they will stage equipment on your property.

Ask for the warranty in writing, including what it covers and what voids it. Ask what happens if the camera pass after curing shows a wrinkle or a blocked lateral opening. A good contractor answers plainly and shows you the footage.

Care after the liner goes in

A lined pipe is not maintenance-free. Keep grease and wipes out of the line, since a new surface can still clog. Schedule a camera check a few years after the work to confirm the liner is intact and the joints are tight. If you see slow drains return, call early, because a problem at an unlined connection is easier to fix before it spreads.

Costs and timelines in general terms

Lining is often compared with digging on cost and time. Lining usually involves less restoration and a shorter disruption, while a long trench takes more time and more cleanup. The right choice depends on length, depth, access and pipe condition. We give you a plan and a price before work starts.
