Bathroom Faucet Drip Repair in Windsor, CT

A dripping bathroom faucet is usually a worn cartridge or washer. Here is how to find the cause, fix it and decide when a plumber is the better call.

Call (860) 397-8103

By the Windsor 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026 · Updated November 12, 2026

A dripping faucet is more than an annoyance. The drip wastes water, and the worn part that causes it keeps wearing, which can damage the valve seat and turn a cheap fix into a replacement. Most bathroom faucet drips are simple to fix once you know what type of faucet you have. Here is how to work it out.

Identify your faucet type

TypeHow to recognize itCommon failed part
CompressionTwo handles that you turn several timesRubber washer and seat
CartridgeOne or two handles that move smoothlyThe cartridge or its O-rings
BallSingle handle over a rounded capSprings and seals
Ceramic discHandle turns a quarter or half turnCeramic cartridge and seals

Basic repair steps

Turn off the water at the shutoff valves under the sink and open the faucet to drain it. Plug the drain so small parts do not fall in. Remove the handle, usually by prying off a decorative cap and loosening a set screw. Remove the retaining nut or clip, then pull out the cartridge or the stem.

Compare the old part to a new one before you buy. Take the old part to a hardware store if you can, since brands vary. Clean the valve body, install the new part, reassemble the handle and turn the water on slowly.

Why it still drips after the repair

If the faucet still drips, the valve seat may be pitted. On a compression faucet, a seat dressing tool can smooth it. On other types, a damaged body may mean replacement is the better choice. Mineral buildup can also keep a new part from sealing, so clean the valve body thoroughly.

A drip from the base of the spout or the handle points to worn O-rings. A drip under the sink points to a supply line or a shutoff valve, which is a different job.

Water, bills and small drips in Windsor

Windsor is served by the MDC, which publishes an annual water quality report. A faucet that scales up quickly may point to mineral content in your water, and a test of your tap tells you more than a guess. If scale keeps fouling cartridges, replacing parts more often is the price.

A drip adds up on a water bill. If your bill is high and the faucet is not the only suspect, see leak detection to check for a larger leak, such as a running toilet or a hidden line.

When to replace the faucet instead

Replace the faucet if it is old and parts are hard to find, if the finish is corroded, if the body is cracked or if the faucet is loose in the sink. A new faucet with a modern cartridge is easier to service and often more efficient. While you are replacing it, swap the supply lines and check the shutoff valves.

If a shutoff valve will not close or leaks when you turn it, call a plumber. Forcing an old valve can break it and flood the cabinet.

Related fixes

A leaking sink drain or trap is a separate issue, and slow drains need drain cleaning. Our faucet repair page covers kitchen and bathroom faucets, and garbage disposal service covers the under-sink unit.

Tools and parts to gather

Set aside about an hour for a first repair and work near a floor towel. Close the shutoff valves under the sink first, then open the faucet to drain the lines and relieve pressure. Put the small parts in a cup in the order you remove them, so reassembly is easy to follow.

You need an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver set, an Allen key set, plumber's grease and a rag or towel to protect the finish. Have a small container for screws, since they roll into drains. Take a photo at each step of disassembly so reassembly is easy.

Buy the replacement part before you take the faucet apart if you can identify the model, or take the old part with you to match it. A hardware store clerk can help with cartridges and O-rings. Keep the shutoff valves within reach in case the work takes longer than expected.

How much a drip costs you

A faucet that drips once a second wastes a surprising amount of water in a month, and hot water drips waste the energy used to heat it. The cost is small each day and adds up over a year. A repair takes less time than most people expect, and it protects the valve seat from damage.

If you cannot fix it right away, place a small container under the spout to track the drip rate. If it is increasing, the part is wearing fast, and the sooner you replace it the cheaper the repair.
