Cleaning a Main Sewer Line in Windsor, CT

A main sewer line carries everything from your house to the street. Here is how a blockage shows up and how a plumber cleans it.

Call (860) 397-8103

By the Windsor 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

Your main sewer line collects every drain in the house and carries it underground to the street. When it clogs, the symptoms are not subtle. Several fixtures drain slowly or back up at once, the lowest drain in the house overflows first and toilets gurgle when you run a tub. Cleaning it is a job for the right equipment, and it is worth knowing what the process involves.

Signs of a main line blockage

Clues that point to the main line

  • ✓
    Multiple fixtures backing up A single slow sink is a branch problem. Several at once is the main.
  • ✓
    Gurgling and bubbling Air pushed back through traps as water stalls.
  • ✓
    Water coming up in the basement floor drain The lowest point shows trouble first.
  • ✓
    Sewage odors in the house or yard Waste is not moving freely.
  • ✓
    Wet or lush patches over the line A leak or overflow outside.

How the line is cleaned

Most cleaning starts at a cleanout, a capped pipe that gives access to the main. A sewer machine feeds a spinning cable with a cutting head through the pipe to break up the blockage. For grease and sludge, high-pressure water jetting scours the walls. We often cable first to restore flow, then jet to clean, then run a camera to verify.

The camera matters. A line that was blocked by grease needs different follow-up than one blocked by roots or a collapse, and the footage shows which. You can watch the monitor and keep the recording.

Who owns what in Windsor

Windsor is an MDC member town, and the MDC provides wastewater services. In general, the utility is responsible for its main in the street, while the property owner is responsible for the lateral from the house to the connection. Ask the MDC where its responsibility begins for your address, because that boundary decides who pays when the blockage is found.

A camera pass helps. If the blockage is in the street main, the footage shows that your lateral is clear, and the MDC takes it from there. State your findings in writing when you report it, and keep the video.

Source type: MDC member-town information.

Why sewer lines clog

Grease coats the pipe and traps solids. Flushed wipes and paper towels catch on rough spots. Roots enter through tiny gaps and thicken. A belly in the pipe holds water and waste that settles. Old clay and cast iron pipes develop scale, cracks and offset joints.

Cleaning treats the symptom. A camera finds the cause. If the cause is a break or an offset joint, sewer line repair is the lasting fix.

What to do while you wait

Stop using water. Do not flush, run the dishwasher or do laundry. Keep the area around a backed-up drain clear and keep children and pets away. Avoid chemical openers, which do not help in a main line and can be hazardous. If sewage reaches the floor, wear gloves and avoid contact.

Find your cleanout, which is usually in the basement near the front wall or just outside by the foundation. Telling the dispatcher where it is speeds up the visit.

Preventing the next backup

Keep grease out of drains, flush only toilet paper and watch tree roots near the line. Consider a camera inspection every few years if your house is older or has mature trees. See sewer camera inspection for what that visit involves.

What the camera footage tells you after cleaning

A clean pipe on camera is a good sign. Look for smooth walls, no standing water and joints that line up. If the footage shows roots at a joint, a hairline crack or a low spot that holds water, you know the clog will come back. Ask the plumber to mark the location on the surface so a repair crew can find it.

Keep the recording with your house records. When you sell, a clean inspection is useful to a buyer, and a documented repair shows the problem was handled.

Warning signs to watch between cleanings

A gurgle after you flush, a slow tub that returns after cleaning or an odor near a floor drain are early signals. A patch of lush grass or damp soil over the line is another. Note the date and what you were doing, since patterns help a plumber find the cause.

If you notice two of these signs, schedule a visit before the line stops completely. A planned cleaning costs less and disrupts less than an emergency call at night.
