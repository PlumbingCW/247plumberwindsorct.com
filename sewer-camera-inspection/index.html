Sewer Camera Inspection in Windsor, CT

A sewer camera inspection in Windsor, CT sends a waterproof camera through your drain line so you can see cracks, roots and sags instead of guessing.

Call (860) 397-8103

A sewer camera inspection is the most direct way to know the condition of an underground drain line. A flexible cable carries a waterproof camera and light through a cleanout. A monitor shows the pipe in real time, and the unit records footage. If you are dealing with active sewage backup instead, call (860) 397-8103 for the 24/7 line, and ask about cleaning first.

Planned inspections are quick to schedule. They cost far less than discovering a failed line after closing on a house or after the third backup in a year.

How the visit works

We find a cleanout or pull a toilet if no cleanout exists. The camera travels as far as the line allows, and we watch for the issues above. A locator on the camera head tells us the depth and position from above, which we mark so you know where a repair would start. You receive notes and the recording.

If we find something, we explain whether it needs cleaning, repair or monitoring. Not every crack needs excavation, and some findings are best watched over time with a repeat inspection.

Buying a house in Windsor or nearby

Older neighborhoods in and around Windsor Center, Poquonock and Wilson include homes with original clay or cast iron laterals. Tree roots and soil movement are common reasons those lines age out. A camera inspection before closing can reveal whether you are buying a clean line or a future repair, and it gives you leverage in negotiation.

If the camera shows a problem, see sewer line repair for what comes next. If it shows buildup, sewer line cleaning or hydro jetting may be the right answer.

Limits of an inspection

A camera can only travel through accessible pipe. A full trap, a bend or a collapse may stop it. We tell you where the camera could not go. We also do not make promises about the future condition of the pipe. The footage records what the line looks like on the day we inspect it.

How to use the footage

The recording is more than proof. It helps you compare quotes, since another contractor can review the same footage. It documents the line for a buyer or an insurer and gives you a baseline. A second inspection a few years later shows whether roots or sags have changed.

If you are planning a landscaping project, a patio or a new tree, ask us to mark the line's route on the ground first. Knowing the depth and path keeps you from planting roots over a weak joint or digging through a lateral.

Older buildings and camera inspections

Homes in older parts of town, such as the streets around Broad Street Green in Windsor Center, are a common reason to look at a drain line before deciding on a repair.
