Poquonock is a mill village in the north of Windsor, and it feels more rural than the rest of town. If you live here and have a plumbing problem, call (860) 397-8103 and tell us what you see. We will help you decide what to do next.

The 2015 Windsor plan says Poquonock was a mill village whose paper and textile mills employed over 500 workers at their peak. It also notes that the first shade tent in New England was set up here in 1900, for shade tobacco. Today the plan describes the land use as predominantly residential. Our main page for plumbing in Windsor, CT covers the whole town.

A village shaped by Route 75

The plan calls Route 75, also known as Poquonock Avenue, the spine of development in Poquonock. Small clusters of business zoning run along Route 75 and Tunxis Street, and the plan names Welch Park and River Street Park among local green spaces. The Villages at Poquonock, a newer development, was approved under a special design district.

Poquonock's vision in the 2035 draft plan is a village with historic charm and small-scale mixed use. For a homeowner that means a mix of ages and building types, so describe your house when you call.

River Street and the Farmington River

The 2015 plan notes that River Street runs close to the river and carries several dwellings and mature trees. The Capitol Region hazard mitigation plan lists River Street among Windsor's flood-prone roads. These are town-level findings, and they do not say that any particular basement floods.

If you live near the river, keep a working sump pump, clear floor drains before storms and ask the Windsor building department about your flood zone. For wet basements, see basement waterproofing and sump pump installation and repair.

Water and sewer here: what we know and do not know

The plan says the MDC supplies drinking water to most of Windsor and that only a small share of properties depend on groundwater. It does not say which streets in Poquonock are on public sewer, and we could not confirm that from a source. Ask the Town of Windsor or the MDC about your address before planning work.

If your home has a well or a septic system, tell the plumber when you call. Drain problems and water pressure issues are handled differently on those systems. For well questions, ask the Town of Windsor.
