Why Wilson is different from the rest of Windsor

The town's plan discusses two-family and three-family homes in Wilson. That matters for plumbing because one building can hold several households. A leak in one unit can reach another, and a shared drain can back up into the lowest apartment.

Windsor Avenue and the older housing stock

In its Wilson discussion, the town's plan says older housing can be functionally obsolete, with smaller rooms and fewer bathrooms. It says nothing about pipe materials, and we will not guess. If your house is old, the useful question is what is inside the walls and under the floor. A sewer camera inspection answers that for the drain line.

For a clogged kitchen or bathroom line, start with drain cleaning. If several fixtures back up together, the main line may be the problem, and sewer line cleaning is the next conversation.

Deckers Brook and basement water

Deckers Brook is one of the brooks named in the Capitol Region hazard mitigation plan for Windsor, and the town's own plan notes that buildings in Wilson were demolished for flood control. The Windsor Historical Society has also written about how the Deckers Brook area changed over time.

None of that proves your basement will flood. It is a reason to check the basics. Test a sump pump before heavy rain, keep floor drains clear and ask the Windsor building department whether your address is in a flood zone. If a pump has failed, see sump pump installation and repair.

What the town says about Wilson

The 2035 draft plan describes Wilson as a neighborhood business district with safe streets, everyday services and a range of housing choices. It also mentions a proposed riverfront park at the end of Meadow Road. Plans can change, so check with the town before relying on a project date.
