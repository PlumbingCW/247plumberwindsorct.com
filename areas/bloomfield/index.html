Stormwater, brooks and wet basements in Bloomfield

Most of Bloomfield drains toward the Park River watershed, and a handful of brooks cross the town, including Wash, Tumbledown, Beaman, Griffin and Mill Brook. The state owns four flood control reservoirs in the Park River watershed within town, and the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection and the town share maintenance. That infrastructure helps, but it does not keep every basement dry.

The town's hazard planning documents describe drainage problems in denser areas of older housing, and they record culvert and stormwater work on Applewood Road, East Newberry Road and Ryefield Hollow. If your basement takes on water near any of those streets, a working sump pump with a battery backup is the first line of defense. Persistent seepage is a question for basement waterproofing.

Water and sewer service in Bloomfield

Bloomfield is an MDC member town, which means the MDC supplies its water and handles wastewater. That matters when something goes wrong. A main in the street is the utility's problem, and a lateral on your property is yours. If you are not sure where the boundary is, call the MDC and ask before you dig. We can tell you what we see on a camera, and that footage helps settle the question.

Backups that appear only during heavy rain can mean stormwater is entering the sewer or the line has a break. A camera visit with sewer camera inspection shows which.

Older neighborhoods, newer pipes

Where housing is dense and older, plumbing often has several generations stacked together. Galvanized lines rust shut, early copper pits and cast iron drains scale over. A burst or a repeat clog in those homes is often the start of a larger conversation about repiping or sewer repair, and we walk you through it without pressure.

Near the library and Filley Pond, the town reports that dam removal, a fishway and dredging upstream reduced flooding in front of the library. Tunxis Avenue and Filley Park saw floodplain structures demolished. If your property sits near that part of town, ask us about backwater valves and drain checks.

Before the next storm

Walk your basement after a hard rain and note where water appears. Check that the sump pump runs, that the discharge line is clear and that downspouts empty well away from the house. If your street is near one of the culvert or stormwater projects the town has recorded, such as Applewood Road, East Newberry Road or Ryefield Hollow, keep an eye on street drains for blockage and tell the town if you see debris.
