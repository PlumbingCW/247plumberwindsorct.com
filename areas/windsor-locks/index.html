Two water systems, one town

Water service in Windsor Locks involves more than one utility. State records list both Connecticut Water and the MDC for Windsor Locks, so the answer to who supplies a given street depends on the address. Check your bill to see which one you pay, because that tells you who to call about the main in the street.

The sewer side matters too. The town runs a water pollution control facility and pump stations, and the town's own plan notes that the Dexter and South Main Street pump stations are a concern during floods. When the sewer system is stressed, backups can show up at the lowest drain in a house. A camera visit helps tell a lateral problem from a system problem.

Mill-era homes and what they hide

Windsor Locks has older homes tied to its mill history, including the area around the Montgomery Mill and the canal. Older houses often have cast iron drains, galvanized supply lines and long basement runs that were rerouted over decades. Those are the pipes that fail slowly and then all at once.

If your home is older and you are seeing rust-colored water, repeated clogs or a low-pressure fixture, a plumber should look at the pipe material. Repiping and sewer line repair are covered on their own pages.

Before the next high-water event

Windsor Locks residents near Center Street, Gaylord Drive and Reed Avenue know the pattern. Test your sump pump before the rainy season, keep a battery backup charged and store valuables above floor level. Know where your main shutoff is, and keep sandbags or a water-barrier plan if your home sits in a low area.

If a storm cuts power and water enters the basement, keep clear of wet outlets and call us after you shut off the main. We will help you sort out what needs attention first.

Safe steps before the plumber arrives in Windsor Locks

Stop using drains if sewage is backing up, and keep children and pets away. Do not enter a flooded basement with a panel or outlets in the water. Shut off the main if water is flowing. If you smell gas, leave and call 911. When to call 911 instead of a plumber: gas smell, a person in danger or water on live electrical equipment.
