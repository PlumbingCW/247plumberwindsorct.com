East Hartford is larger and denser than most of our service area, and a plumbing emergency there often involves older pipes, tight basements or a sewer that is working hard. Shut off the main, then call (860) 397-8103. We answer 24/7 and offer same-day appointments when available.

Backwater, sewer backups and the river

East Hartford's hazard planning documents describe a levee system that the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers built after the 1936 and 1938 floods. The town operates it and has a multi-year program of about twenty-one million dollars to improve it. Behind the levee, the Connecticut River can still push backwater up local brooks. The Porter Brook neighborhood and the Milbrook area near the Senior Center have seen backwater flooding.

In 2020 the town installed a backflow prevention device at the Porter Brook outfall of the Hollister Drive storm drainage system, to keep river water from flowing back into the neighborhood. That is the same idea we use inside homes. A backwater valve on a sewer lateral stops sewage from coming back up a pipe when the system is overloaded. If you have had a backup, ask about one.

Stormwater streets and what they tell you

The town lists Burnside Avenue, Arbutus Street and Cummings Street among streets that flood from stormwater. The Hockanum River overwhelms drainage mainly east of Scotland Road. The Burnham Brook area near University Drive has flooded, and Silver Lane Plaza sits partly within Willow Brook flood zones.

A basement in those areas can fill from groundwater, a failed pump or a backed-up sewer, and the fix differs. We check the sump pump, the floor drains and the lateral. A camera inspection shows whether the line is the problem.

Old housing, tight spaces

Older East Hartford neighborhoods, including some built in the flood zone before current rules, have homes with long histories of plumbing repairs. Tight basements and shared walls complicate work, and we plan access before we cut. In multi-family buildings, a leak can affect neighbors, so we document what we see.

Water and sewer service here comes from the MDC, which supplies East Hartford as a member town. That helps when you need to know who handles the main. For business properties, see commercial plumbing.

Protecting a basement behind the levee

Even with the levee system and its improvements, East Hartford neighborhoods can see stormwater and backwater. Keep floor drains clear and test your sump pump each season. Consider a backwater valve on the sewer lateral if you have had sewage come up during storms or high river levels.

Move stored items off the floor, and keep water heaters and electrical equipment raised where possible. If water does come in, stay away from wet outlets and call us after you shut off the main.
