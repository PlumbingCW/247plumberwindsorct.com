Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Windsor, CT

Water heater replacement in Windsor, CT is the right answer when a tank leaks or a unit is too far gone to repair. Failed tanks get 24/7 attention.

Call (860) 397-8103

A leaking tank cannot be patched. Once the steel liner fails, water escapes and the tank is finished. If you see water around the base, shut off the cold water supply to the heater, switch off the breaker for an electric unit or set a gas unit to pilot or off, and call (860) 397-8103. We answer 24/7, and same-day replacement is offered when available.

Not every no-hot-water call means replacement. A tripped breaker, a failed element or a bad thermostat is a cheaper fix, and we check before recommending a new tank.

When a failed water heater is an emergency

A small drip from a fitting can wait for a daytime appointment. A steady leak from the tank body cannot, because a full tank holds enough water to soak a finished basement. Water near the electric panel, a smell of gas or scorching around a gas unit changes this from inconvenient to urgent.

For gas, if you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us. Safety comes first.

Choosing the replacement

The common choices are a standard gas or electric tank, a tankless unit and a hybrid heat pump model. Standard tanks are the quickest to swap and fit most homes. Tankless saves space and delivers continuous hot water, but the install can need larger gas lines and venting. Hybrids use less electricity, need space and airflow, and add upfront cost.

Fuel and venting drive most decisions. If your home has gas service, a new gas unit usually keeps the install simple. If it is all electric, a hybrid may cut running costs. See tankless water heaters and water heater installation for details on the alternatives.

What replacement involves

We shut off and drain the old tank, disconnect gas or electric, supply lines and venting, and remove the unit. The new heater goes in with new connectors, a proper relief valve with discharge line, and an expansion tank where the system calls for one. We check venting for gas models and test for leaks and combustion safety before leaving.

Permit and inspection rules vary, so ask the Windsor building department what applies to a replacement. Gas work requires a licensed professional, and we handle it that way.

Windsor homes and basements near water

Many Windsor homes keep the water heater in the basement, and some basements sit in areas the town flags for flooding. The town's hazard planning documents list Batchelder Road, Pleasant Street and River Street among flood-prone areas, and Windsor restricts new structures in 100-year floodplains. Where flooding is a risk, raising the heater on a stand and avoiding electric controls at floor level is smart design.

A leaking tank in a finished basement also does damage fast, so replacing an old unit before it fails often costs less than waiting. If yours is more than ten years old, ask for an inspection.

Gas, electric and hybrid models in your house

Gas units need a flue and combustion air, and the flue must slope and seal properly. Electric units need the right breaker and wire gauge. Hybrid heat pump units need clearance, a condensate drain and enough air volume. We check each before we quote, because a model that does not fit the space is a poor buy at any price.

Code and permits for a new tank

A replacement heater is regulated work. Connecticut's State Building Code uses the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as its plumbing standard. The 2022 state code adopts the 2021 IPC, and the state is working on a newer edition. Ask the Windsor building department which permit and inspection apply to your swap.
