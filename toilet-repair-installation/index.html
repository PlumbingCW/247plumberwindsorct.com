Emergency Toilet Repair in Windsor, CT

Toilet repair in Windsor, CT covers overflows, leaks at the base, constant running and full replacements. When one will not stop overflowing, we are on the 24/7 line.

Call (860) 397-8103

An overflowing toilet needs two quick moves. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. That cuts the water. Then call (860) 397-8103 if it will not clear. We answer 24/7, and same-day appointments are offered when available. Do not keep flushing, which only adds water to a blocked line.

A toilet that overflows once may only need an auger. One that overflows repeatedly can point to a bigger problem in the drain, and a plumber should look at the line.

When a toilet problem is an emergency

Water on the bathroom floor that you cannot stop, sewage backing up from the bowl or other drains gurgling at the same time all warrant an urgent call. If water is dripping through the ceiling below, close the main shutoff. A toilet leak on an upper floor can soak a ceiling in minutes.

If several fixtures back up together, the clog is likely in the main drain, and drain cleaning is the right service.

The flange and the floor underneath

A toilet leak at the base is rarely just a wax ring. Water that seeps for months rots the subfloor and corrodes the flange, the ring that holds the toilet to the drain. We check the floor for softness and the flange for cracks before we reset the toilet. A new wax ring on a broken flange leaks again within weeks.

If we find rot, we tell you what we see. Subfloor repair is a carpentry job, but we can mark the area and describe what is needed so you can have it done properly.

Installing a new toilet

A new toilet is a simple swap when the plumbing is sound. We measure the rough-in, the distance from the wall to the center of the drain, so the new toilet fits. We replace the supply line and shutoff valve if they are old, set the toilet on a new seal and check for leaks and rocking.

Water-efficient models are widely available, and a taller bowl can be more comfortable. A toilet that clogs often may be a poor design, not a drain problem, so we can suggest models with strong flush performance.

Older homes and shared drain lines

Older Windsor homes may have toilets on cast iron stacks or on branch lines that run long distances in the basement. Frequent clogs in those homes sometimes come from the drain, not the toilet. A camera inspection shows whether something is stuck in the line, and sewer camera inspection is the next step when a toilet clogs repeatedly.

Basement toilets, often called up-flush or sewage ejector toilets, use a pump. When those fail, water can back up into the pit or the bathroom, and they deserve a prompt visit.

Choosing a replacement toilet

Measure the rough-in, the distance from the wall to the center of the drain bolts. Most are twelve inches, though some are ten or fourteen. Decide on a round or elongated bowl and a standard or taller seat height. Look for a flush that clears waste in a single flush and a glazed trapway that resists clogs.

Dual flush models save water. Pressure-assist models flush forcefully but are louder. A plumber can help you pick one that fits your space and your drain, and set it on a new seal so it does not rock or leak.
