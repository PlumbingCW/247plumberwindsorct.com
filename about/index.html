About Windsor 24/7 Plumbing

We answer plumbing emergency calls around the clock for Windsor, CT and nearby towns, and we dispatch licensed, insured plumbers.

Call (860) 397-8103

What we do

Windsor 24/7 Plumbing is a local emergency plumbing line. When you call (860) 397-8103, someone answers at any hour. You describe the problem, we tell you what to do while you wait and we arrange a licensed, insured plumber for you. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

We keep the work simple. You get a clear price before work starts, an explanation of what failed and a repair that is tested before the plumber leaves.

Where we are

Our office is at 1001 Day Hill Rd in Windsor, in the corporate and industrial office area known as the Day Hill Road corridor. From there we serve Windsor and nearby towns, including Bloomfield, Windsor Locks, South Windsor, East Granby and East Hartford.

How to check who is working on your home

Any plumber who works in Connecticut can be checked on the Department of Consumer Protection's eLicense lookup. Search by name or license number, then read the credential status. It takes a minute and applies to every plumber you consider, including us. You can open the lookup at elicense.ct.gov.

What we will not do

We do not invent reviews or post numbers we cannot back. We do not promise arrival times, because traffic, weather and other emergencies change them. We tell you what is possible when you call, and we do not push work you do not need.

What happens when you call

A person answers and asks four things: your address, what you see, whether water is still running and whether sewage or gas is involved. Those answers decide what we tell you first. If water is flowing, we walk you to the main shutoff. If the problem is a backed-up drain, we tell you what to stop using so it does not get worse.

Then we arrange a licensed, insured plumber and give you the next step. We do not promise an arrival time on the phone, because we cannot control traffic or how many emergencies are open at once. We tell you what is realistic for that night and we stay with you until the plumber is on the way.

What a visit includes

The plumber finds the cause before quoting. You get a price in plain terms before work begins, and nothing starts without your approval. After the repair, the plumber tests it, so you see the fixture or line working before the visit ends. If the problem needs a second visit, such as a part that has to be ordered, you are told why.

For leaks and sewer problems, a camera or a moisture check may come first, since guessing at a buried line wastes money. You can read how that works on our leak detection and sewer camera inspection pages.

Our principles

Plumbing emergencies make people vulnerable to pressure, so we set rules for ourselves. We explain the problem in words you can repeat to your insurer. We give you options when more than one repair is reasonable. We say so when a fix can wait until morning and when it cannot.

We also keep our pages honest. Every service page says what the work involves and what affects the price. We do not publish a flat price for a job that depends on access, pipe material and depth.

If you are comparing plumbers, ask the same questions of each one. Who will arrive, how is the price set, what is tested before the visit ends and what is the plan if the first repair does not hold. A plumber who answers plainly is usually worth your call.

Local resources worth knowing

Some questions are better answered by the people who run the system. The Metropolitan District Commission supplies water in Windsor, Bloomfield and East Hartford, so ask the MDC about water quality and service questions. Call 811 before any digging project, such as a new sump discharge or yard line. Ask the Windsor building department which permits a project needs.

How we write about local conditions

Windsor's rivers, brooks and utilities affect plumbing, and we use official sources for local facts, such as the MDC and the Capitol Region Council of Governments hazard mitigation plan. If we cannot verify a number, such as water hardness, we say to test your tap or ask your utility. You can read more on our plumbing guides and services pages.
