Emergency Drain Cleaning in Windsor, CT

Drain cleaning in Windsor, CT clears slow sinks, stopped tubs and backed-up main lines, with 24/7 service when a clog will not wait for morning.

A clogged drain is an emergency when water is rising, when more than one fixture is backing up, or when sewage is coming up through a floor drain or tub. Stop running water, do not flush, and call (860) 397-8103. We answer 24/7, so nights and weekends are covered. Keep people and pets out of the wet area until it is cleaned up.

A single slow sink is a different story. It usually means a partial clog in the trap or branch line, and it is much cheaper to clear before it stops completely.

Sewage backup: what to do first

When several drains gurgle or back up together, the clog is probably in the main line, not in a single fixture. Stop using water, including toilets and washing machines. Avoid chemical drain openers, since they can damage pipes and are dangerous for the plumber who has to work around them.

Floor drains in basements are often the first place sewage shows up, because they sit lowest. If sewage reaches the floor, treat the area as contaminated. Use gloves, keep children away and do not mix cleaners.

How we clear a drain

We choose the method based on what is blocking the line. A hand auger handles a trap clog. A motorized cable with the right cutter head clears hair, grease and light roots in the branch lines. For heavy buildup or repeated clogs, hydro jetting scours the pipe walls, and you can read about it on our hydro jetting page.

When a clog returns within weeks, cleaning alone is not enough. We send a camera through the line to find out why, whether that is roots in a joint, a sag in the pipe or a broken section. You see the footage, and the repair options follow from it.

Windsor sewer lines and what affects them

Windsor is an MDC member town for both water and sewer service, and your building drain connects to the MDC sewer through a lateral running to the street. A clog in the lateral is usually your responsibility up to the connection, while the street main is the utility's. If you are not sure, ask the MDC where its responsibility starts.

Mature trees along older streets near Windsor Center and Palisado Avenue are a common root source. Roots find tiny cracks and joints, then thicken until water cannot pass. Heavy storms also stress the system, and flood-prone areas can see backups when the sewer is overloaded. If a backup happens only during heavy rain, tell us.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen sinks clog more than any other fixture because of grease, soap film and food particles. Grease cools inside the pipe and traps everything that follows, so a sink that drains slowly today often stops completely next month. We cable the trap arm and the branch line, then flush the pipe with hot water so the film goes too.

A kitchen sink with a disposal or a dishwasher connection adds more places for waste to catch. If the sink backs up into the second basin, the clog is past the disposal. A leak in a drain line under the floor is a different problem, and a camera or pressure test confirms it.

Keeping drains clear

Do not pour grease or coffee grounds down the kitchen sink. Use a hair catcher in the tub, and flush only toilet paper. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper. A cleaning visit every year or two makes sense for homes with old cast iron or a history of roots, and drain cleaning in the South Windsor and Bloomfield areas follows the same approach.
